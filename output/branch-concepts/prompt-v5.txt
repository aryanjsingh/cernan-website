== STYLE: VECTOR / SVG ==
- Flat and semi-flat vector shapes, clean bezier outlines, consistent stroke weights (2-6 px at this resolution), rounded caps.
- Limited palette, matched to the sky: deep navy ink #0b1a2b for the limb, white #ffffff, pale cyan #bff0ff, sky blues, two or three moss/leaf greens (#2f7d4f, #59a96a, #a9d18e), and orange #ff7a2f for error signals.
- Shading only with flat tones, simple gradients or fine stipple dots. No photographic textures, no realistic bark, no depth-of-field blur, no noise, no 3D render look.
- Clean, minimal, premium: it should look like a high-end motion-design frame, e.g. Stripe or Linear illustration quality.

== THE SUBJECT ==
One limb enters from OFF-SCREEN at the RIGHT EDGE (no ground, no trunk base), centre line between y=300 and y=650 at the edge, drawn about 220-320 px thick there. It branches leftward and widens into a canopy, tips reaching x = 1150-1500. Branching is irregular and natural: uneven angles, different lengths, one or two crossing or sagging, asymmetric. Never evenly spaced or mirrored.
- NEURONS: round nodes at the forks (circles with a ring), different sizes, decreasing toward the tips. The branch tips end in small output nodes.
- OVERGROWTH: stylised moss cushions along the tops of limbs (flat green blobs with stipple texture), simple vector leaves and ivy, a few small ferns, and stylised hanging moss / vines as fine vertical lines dropping down on the right half, longest to about y = 1500.
- CHANNELS: every branch has a clean thin line running along it, node to node: the path the signals travel.

== BACKPROPAGATION MUST BE VISIBLE ==
The frame captures the network mid-training, so a viewer understands it at a glance:
- FORWARD PASS: pale cyan/white glowing pulses (small capsules with soft glow and a short motion trail) travelling along the channels from the right-edge input toward the tips, on some branches.
- LOSS: a few output nodes at the tips glow orange, with a soft orange halo.
- BACKWARD PASS: orange pulses travelling back from those tips along the channels toward the right edge, the lit channel segments getting thicker and brighter where more error flows (thicker near the trunk).
- Use small motion trails and direction cues (dashed segments, tiny arrow chevrons) so the direction of flow is obvious. No text, labels, numbers or formulas.
